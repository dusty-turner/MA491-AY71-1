About

I teach probability, statistics, and mathematical modeling to cadets at West Point, and I build interactive apps that let them turn the knobs on a concept instead of reading about it. My research is on how the Army flies and maintains its aircraft: what readiness numbers actually measure, how units decide to launch, and when a maintenance technology stops paying for itself.

Before teaching I spent a decade in Army finance and operations research, resourcing units from a military police brigade in the Indo-Pacific to a three-star special operations headquarters. I hold a PhD in Operations Research from NC State, along with master's degrees in statistics and operations research, and a BS in Economics from West Point.

The Blueprint
The Question

A helicopter fleet is asked to do two things that fight each other. Fly it hard and it earns the training hours the unit is graded on. Fly it hard and parts wear out, so the readiness rate the unit is also graded on falls. The Army manages this with one headline number, the mission-capable rate, and that number cannot tell a fleet that is healthy from a fleet that is idle.

The Model

Readiness and utilization come out of one coupled system, so I model them jointly rather than optimizing either alone. On any given day a unit's dispatch decision balances the flight hours it gains against the expected maintenance it incurs:

\[ \max_{u_t} \; \sum_{t=1}^{T} \big[\, v(u_t) - c\big(E[D_{t+1} \mid u_t]\big) \,\big] \]

where \(u_t\) is aircraft flown on day \(t\), \(v(\cdot)\) the training value earned, and \(D_{t+1}\) the downtime that flying induces tomorrow. The interesting behavior lives in that lag: today's value is certain, tomorrow's cost is not.

Prognostics

Predictive maintenance promises to forecast remaining useful life and schedule the repair before the failure. Its payoff depends on how accurate that forecast is, and accuracy is expensive. Past a point, buying more of it changes nothing a commander would do differently.

The Teaching Connection

Every Forge app grew out of the same habit this research runs on: if you cannot move the input and watch the answer respond, you do not yet understand the model.
